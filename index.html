Problem Statement SIH26051

DESIGN THE SHELTER. NOT THE HEATER.

In high-altitude Himalayan cold deserts like Ladakh, winter ambient temperatures plunge to -22°C to -35°C. Conventional shelters rely on massive fossil kerosene and diesel heating — burning thousands of liters per season, generating dangerous indoor carbon monoxide, and placing an immense logistical burden on airlift helicopters (Mi-17).

PHYSICS ASSUMPTIONS & EQUATIONS

1. Transient Energy Conservation

$$C_{eff} \frac{dT_{in}}{dt} = Q_{solar} + Q_{int} - \sum UA (T_{in} - T_{amb}) - Q_{inf} - Q_{storage}$$

Solved using explicit forward Euler numerical integration at \(\Delta t = 300\text{ s}\) across 3 consecutive diurnal warm-up cycles to achieve steady-periodic state.

2. High Altitude Air Properties

At 3,505m elevation (Leh, Ladakh):

$$P = 66.5\text{ kPa}, \quad \rho_{air} = 0.85\text{ kg/m}^3$$

Air density derating reduces the volumetric heat capacity of infiltration air compared to sea-level design standards.

3. Ground Snow Albedo

Ground snow reflectance is modeled with albedo \(\rho_g = 0.60\), enhancing solar irradiance on the vertical south facade by up to 25% during clear winter days.

4. Phase Change Material (PCM)

Apparent heat capacity method simulates latent enthalpy absorption over a \(3.0^\circ\text{C}\) melting interval around nominal transition temperature (\(21^\circ\text{C}\)).

5. Model Limitations

Reduced-order model assumes well-mixed air temperature. Detailed structural corner thermal bridging and microclimate turbulent eddies require full 3D CFD/FEA refinement.
